# Q(labs) application notes

## 1. Describe your company in a single sentence

They asked for one sentence. Giving them roughly 400 words ignores the instruction and reads badly before the reviewers have read the content. Lead with this sentence:

> Q9 Labs is an AI-native R&D company building products that eliminate core business bottlenecks — voice agents (Ava), AI-driven hiring (Recruiter), unified task-and-communication (Track, OSS), and realtime video collaboration (Chalk, OSS).

If the form allows longer answers, follow with three or four tight lines maximum, with one line per product. Drop “recursive self-improvement,” “build with sincerity,” and the counseling line from this answer; save that detail for later questions. Also cut “(capital is what's holding us back)” because it frames the company as blocked rather than resourceful.

## 2. Why are you working on this idea?

Use a tighter version:

> We're working on this because the way businesses operate is fundamentally broken. You'd think core business processes would have been streamlined over the past two decades. The reality couldn't be further from it. We believe radical transformation is needed: a future where people don't build PowerPoints or juggle tasks in their heads, where a single employee does the work of a hundred, where agents work 24/7, and where humanity innovates in leaps because we've freed ourselves from tedium. Today this reality belongs to a small few. We're building to make it the norm.

## 3. How far along are you?

“Actively being used by a few users” is the weakest phrase in the application. Replace vague wording with concrete numbers wherever available: paying customers, interviews conducted by Recruiter, hires made, or revenue. Suggested wording:

> All four products are in production with active users. Ava and Recruiter are stable. Track is in beta, and Chalk is undergoing a v2 rebuild. Recruiter has screened thousands of candidates and conducted hundreds of AI interviews. We've had customers hire within 24 hours of posting.

## 4. Equity

“Not formal” is a red flag because it signals unresolved founder risk. Reframe the arrangement as intentional and imminent:

> 50/50 between myself (co-founder, CTO) and my co-founder (CEO).

## 5. Something you're proud of

They want one concrete achievement. “I'm proud of everything” dodges the question, so lead with Recruiter:

> I'm most proud of Recruiter. We built an AI interviewer that users consistently tell us conducts better first-round interviews than an average human recruiter. One customer opened a job posting and made a hire in under 24 hours. Track is a close second: the insight that task management should be an artifact of conversation, not a separate tool, is the best product idea I've had and has massive potential. Most of all, we did it as a small team outside SF, and bootstrapped.
